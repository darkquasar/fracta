# Fracta License History

Fracta is currently released under the **Functional Source License v1.1
with Apache License 2.0 future grant** ([FSL-1.1-ALv2](./LICENSE)).

The project has used the following licenses over time:

| Commit range | License | Period |
|---|---|---|
| `dd8a11d` (initial commit) — `90b1864` | MIT | 2026-04-12 to 2026-05-20 |
| Commits after `90b1864` | FSL-1.1-ALv2 | 2026-05-20 — present |

## What this means

Snapshots of Fracta at any commit up to and including `90b1864` **remain
MIT-licensed in perpetuity at those specific revisions**, as required by
the irrevocable nature of the MIT grant. Anyone who obtained a copy of
the Software at those revisions retains an MIT license to that copy.

All commits introduced after `90b1864` — including all new releases of
Fracta from this point forward — are released under FSL-1.1-ALv2. Each
release auto-converts to the Apache License 2.0 on the second
anniversary of its publication, per the standard FSL terms.

The relicense reflects the project's evolution from an early-stage solo
codebase to a source-available project intended to support a long-term
commercial roadmap. The FSL was chosen because it explicitly permits
internal use, modification, and redistribution by any organization,
while reserving competing hosted-service offerings for the author and
his successors-in-interest. See [`COMMERCIAL.md`](./COMMERCIAL.md) for
details on what the FSL permits and when a commercial license is needed.

## Why no MIT bridge after this point

The MIT license is irrevocable for the snapshots it covered. The FSL
applies prospectively only — i.e. to commits made on or after the
relicense date. Existing MIT-licensed snapshots are not retroactively
modified, removed, or converted; doing so is neither legally possible
nor desirable. The intent of this `LICENSE-HISTORY.md` is to make the
boundary unambiguous for downstream users, contributors, and legal
reviewers.
