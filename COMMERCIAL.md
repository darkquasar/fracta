## What the FSL permits at no charge

The examples below are illustrative, **not exhaustive**. Many real-world
uses fall under "Permitted Purpose" in the LICENSE; the cases below are
common patterns that almost always qualify.

You can use Fracta freely under the FSL in any of the following situations:

- **Internal use, any organization size.** Run Fracta on your own
  infrastructure to support your own work — security operations,
  knowledge management, internal automation, research, threat hunting,
  whatever — regardless of whether your organization is a charity, a
  startup, a Fortune 500 enterprise, or anything in between.
- **Modifying Fracta for your own use.** Patch bugs, customize the
  configuration, build internal extensions or strategies. Your
  modifications remain subject to the FSL.
- **Building strategies, plugins, and integrations on top of Fracta.**
  These are derivative works and are also covered by the FSL by default.
- **Using Fracta to do your own work for clients, internally.** For
  example, a consultancy may run Fracta on its own laptops or servers to
  investigate data and produce reports for clients, provided that the
  deliverable to the client is the analysis, report, or recommendations —
  not Fracta itself.
- **Helping a client deploy Fracta in their own environment, where the
  client uses Fracta directly and is themselves an FSL licensee** in
  compliance with these terms. This is the "professional services to a
  licensee" carve-out in the FSL.
- **Non-commercial education and research.**

**Important: this list is illustrative, not exhaustive.** Any use of
Fracta that is not a "Competing Use" as defined in the LICENSE is
permitted. If your situation isn't covered by the examples above, the
LICENSE text still controls — the test is whether your use is a
Competing Use, not whether it appears on this list.

## What requires a commercial license

Again, these examples are **illustrative, not exhaustive**. The
controlling test is the "Competing Use" definition in the LICENSE: a
commercial product or service that substitutes for Fracta, substitutes
for any commercial product or service the author offers using Fracta, or
offers the same or substantially similar functionality as Fracta.

Common patterns that require a commercial license include:

- **Offering Fracta (or a fork, repackage, or derivative) as a hosted
  product or managed service to customers.** Whether you call it a SaaS,
  a managed-service offering, or a "platform" — if customers are paying
  to access Fracta-as-a-service, you need a commercial license.
- **Embedding Fracta into a commercial product or service such that
  Fracta is a substantial portion of what you're selling.** Rule of
  thumb: if removing Fracta from your offering would fundamentally change
  what you're selling, you probably need a commercial license.
- **Productizing Fracta as a branded consulting offering** — e.g.
  "[Your Firm]'s Swarm Threat Platform powered by Fracta."
- **Any other arrangement that constitutes a "Competing Use"** under the
  LICENSE, even if it is not specifically listed above. The list here is
  not a safe harbor for uses that fall outside it but still meet the
  Competing Use definition.

If your intended use does not appear on either list but it might involve
making Fracta available to others in a commercial context, the safest
approach is to apply the test in the LICENSE directly — or just ask. See
"How to request a commercial license" below.

please reach out. Most real-world uses are on the free side of the line,
and a brief email exchange is faster and friendlier than guessing wrong.
